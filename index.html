      How this was made

      Who's counted

      Every show gets a main cast: the performers credited as series regulars in season 1. Season 1 keeps the rule simple and captures each show as it launched, though it misses people who joined later (Walton Goggins in Justified, Michael B. Jordan in Friday Night Lights). Show notes point those out.

      The leads are a subset of that cast, picked by one test: is the show built around this character? That covers a title role, the point-of-view character, a two-hander, or a clearly defined core group like the Roys in Succession or the Fishers in Six Feet Under. When no single character or core dominates, as in Community, The OA, The Wire or Lost, the whole main cast counts as leads.

      For documentary and reality series, the host, narrator and regular judges make up the cast. Black Mirror has no recurring cast and is left out. The White Lotus uses its season 1 cast, which is an imperfect fit for an anthology. Band of Brothers and Twin Peaks: The Return had no series regulars, so their notes explain who's counted.

      How race and gender were coded

      Race and gender follow how each performer is publicly identified. Latino/Hispanic and Middle Eastern/North African are treated as their own categories. Performers with parents of different races are coded as multiracial, with the specifics listed. Nonbinary performers have their own gender category. A handful of performers whose background couldn't be confirmed are marked "Not confirmed" rather than guessed at.

      A few classifications are genuinely debatable (Anya Taylor-Joy, Louis C.K., Stephen Graham), and each is flagged in its show's notes.

      Caveats

      • Ranks come from the order of the list; premiere years are for the first season.
      • Cast sizes vary widely, from one regular to nearly twenty. The "shows weighted equally" bars give each show the same total weight, split evenly among its performers, so big ensembles don't swamp the totals.
      • Season 1 cast lists were compiled by hand and are worth spot-checking against each show's credits before you rely on them.
      • All of this sits in one data array in this page's source, so any call you disagree with is a one-line change.

      Show selections and rankings: The New York Times, "The 100 Best TV Shows of the 21st Century." Lead selection and race and gender coding were done for this page and are open to revision.
