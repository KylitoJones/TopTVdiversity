      How this was made

      Who counts as a lead

      The Times ranked shows, not performers, so deciding who "leads" a show is a judgment call. Most shows here get one or two leads: the character the story is built around, or a true two-hander. Ensemble shows get up to four, drawn from the top-billed cast. For documentary and reality series, the host, narrator or judges count as the lead. The two anthologies with no fixed cast, Black Mirror and The White Lotus, are left out of the lead counts.

      Where a call was close, the show's detail panel says so. Ensembles like Lost, Community and Stranger Things have diverse supporting casts that a four-lead limit leaves out.

      How race and gender were coded

      Race and gender follow how each performer is publicly identified. Latino/Hispanic is treated as its own category. Performers with parents of different races are coded as multiracial, with the specifics listed. A few classifications are genuinely debatable (Anya Taylor-Joy, Louis C.K., Stephen Graham); each is flagged in its show's notes.

      Caveats

      • Ranks come from the order of the list; premiere years are for the first season.
      • Lead counts shape the per-lead totals: a four-person ensemble adds four leads, a solo show one. The show-by-show chart counts each series once to balance that.
      • All of this sits in one data array at the bottom of this page's source, so any call you disagree with is a one-line change.

      Show selections and rankings: The New York Times, "The 100 Best TV Shows of the 21st Century." Lead selection and race and gender coding were done for this page and are open to revision.
